# Complete Guide to Applying for an AFSL in Australia (Step-by-Step)

---

## Raw post_content (HTML stripped, for reference)

To apply for an Australian Financial Services Licence (AFSL licence) is an important step. With it, your business would have legal permission to work and deliver its financial services.

Providing advice to customers, offering financial products, and managing investments are all regulated by Australian laws. These activities require you to obtain an AFSL licence. In addition, getting an AFSL can make you more trustworthy to your clients.

This guide explains the AFS Licence Application process step by step. It is easy to understand. It also covers key AFSL compliance requirements. It also explains ongoing AFS licence obligations.
What Is An AFSL Licence?

An AFSL is an Australian Financial Services Licence. With it, you are entitled to conduct certain activities. These can include:

- Financial product advice

- Financial product dealing

- Portfolios management

- Other financial services


Without an AFSL licence, offering these services in Australia is not permitted. Beyond legality, it also demonstrates professionalism, credibility, and adherence to industry standards.
Who Needs an AFSL?

Generally speaking, if your business provides any type of financial service listed above, you need to get an AFSL licence. For example:

- Providing financial product advice

- Deals in financial products (buying/selling on behalf of clients)

- Operating a managed investment scheme

- Custody or deposit taking services

- Offering traditional trustee company services


If you are unsure, assess your services carefully. You can also consult an expert before you proceed with the AFS Licence Application.
AFSL Step-by-Step Guide

Applying for an AFSL licence is not just filling out a form. It means proving your business can operate responsibly. You must manage risks and meet strict regulatory standards. Below is a more detailed breakdown of the AFS Licence Application process.
Step 1: Identify The Services Your Business Will Provide Under Your Licence

It is important to know what financial services you will offer. You must also prove your business can provide them under current laws. Start by identifying exactly what services your business will provide under the Australian financial services licence.

For example:

- Do you provide financial advice?

- Do you buy/sell financial products for your customers?

- Do you conduct investments or fund management?

- Wholesale or retail clients?


This step is important since you will only be allowed to provide the type of service indicated in your licence.
Step 2: Check Whether Your Business Meets Necessary Requirements

Your business needs to possess specific capabilities before you can apply for an AFSL licence application. These include, but are not limited to the following:

- Organisational competence – Do you have the right people and expertise?

- Financial capacity – Can your business sustain operations?

- Risk management systems – Do you have controls in place?

- Ability to comply with regulatory standards – Can you meet ongoing AFSL compliance?


Thus, it isn't just about providing information to regulators, but also about being capable to perform financial services. 

For detailed regulatory guidance, refer to ASIC’s RG 1 AFS Licensing Kit: Part 1 – Applying for and varying an AFS licence, which outlines key requirements and AFSL compliance expectations.
Step 3: Appoint Suitable Responsible Managers

Your AFSL licence will depend heavily on responsible managers. They will be responsible for making sure that your business always complies with regulatory requirements.

In particular, a responsible manager:

- Has sufficient industry experience

- Understands all compliance-related matters

- Is actively involved in the business operation


Their role is to ensure your company meets AFSL compliance standards at all times. Weak or inexperienced managers are one of the most common reasons for application delays or rejection.
Step 4: Establish a Proper Compliance System

To prove to regulators that you will meet all compliance requirements in future, you should build your compliance system.

Its components include:

- Policies and procedures

- Risk management systems

- Monitoring procedures

- Staff training programs


This step directly impacts both your application approval and your ability to meet AFSL compliance after licensing.

Step 5: Prepare All Necessary Documents

As a next step in your AFS License Application process, it is necessary to prepare all required documentation. Here is a list of materials you are expected to submit:

- Description of your business and services provided

- Financial statements and forecasts

- Compliance manuals and other policies

- Documentation related to responsible managers (curriculum vitae, qualifications, etc.)

- Organisational structure of your business


Poor-quality documents can make you spend additional weeks trying to receive your AFSL.
Step 6: Submit Your AFS Licence Application

Once you've done the first five steps, you can proceed to submission of your AFS License Application through ASIC’s eLicensing portal.

Some tips here:

- Check all the documents before sending them

- Make sure all the documents are consistent

- Explain the model of your business


All these will speed up the process of receiving your AFS licence.
Step 7: Respond to Questions Promptly

It's possible that the regulatory body might have some doubts and will ask you about certain aspects of your business operations or plans.

Typical questions are:

- Clarification of services you'll provide

- Details about compliance systems

- Proofs of organisational capability


Thus, timely answering of all questions will facilitate further process greatly.
Step 8: Licence Approval and Implementation of Your Plan

Once approved, you will receive your AFSL licence, allowing you to legally operate under an Australian financial services licence.

At this stage, your focus should shift to:

- Implementing your compliance systems

- Training your team

- Delivering services within your approved scope


Understanding AFSL Compliance

After obtaining your Australian financial services licence, maintaining AFSL compliance is essential. Key compliance requirements include:

- Regular reporting and record-keeping

- Staff training and competency updates

- Risk management and internal audits

- Client disclosure and transparency


Failing to meet compliance standards can result in penalties or licence suspension.

FAQ

How do I apply for an AFSL licence?

To apply for an AFSL licence, you must submit an AFS Licence Application to ASIC with all required documentation. This includes business details, responsible managers’ qualifications, compliance policies, and financial statements. You must also demonstrate organisational competence and financial capability to meet AFSL compliance standards.

What are the requirements for getting an Australian financial services licence?

To obtain an Australian financial services licence, you generally need to:

- Establish a legal entity in Australia 

- Register under the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth)

- Hold a valid ABN or ACN

- Have at least one Australian-resident director

- Meet AFSL compliance requirements, including risk management and internal systems


How much does an AFS Licence Application cost?

The cost of an AFS Licence Application varies depending on complexity. ASIC application fees typically range from around $3,721 to $7,537. However, total costs - including professional fees and compliance setup - can range between $10,000 and $60,000 or more.

How long does it take to get an AFSL licence?

ASIC aims to make a decision within 120 days after accepting your AFS Licence Application. However, in practice, it often takes between 4 to 8 months, depending on the complexity of the application and how quickly you respond to queries.

What documents are required for an AFSL licence?

You will need to provide detailed documentation, including:

- Business model and service descriptions

- Financial statements and forecasts

- Compliance frameworks and policies

- Responsible manager qualifications and experience

- Organisational structure


What are ongoing AFS licence obligations?

Once approved, you must meet ongoing AFS licence obligations such as:

- Maintaining adequate financial resources

- Ongoing staff training and competency

- Regular reporting to ASIC

- Implementing risk management systems

- Ensuring client transparency and disclosure


Failure to meet these AFS licence obligations can result in penalties or suspension.
